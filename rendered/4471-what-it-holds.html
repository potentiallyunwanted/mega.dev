Every row is a different fault with a different fix. The queued parser fix closes the first one and leaves the others arriving.

The count the desk has been quoting comes from the exported rows, not the reports. The export ships some reports twice, so the ticket is smaller than it looks — and `uniqueById` in `#lib/kb/causes` exists because of it.

Why one ticket held more than one

Decision 0001 merges a report into an open ticket when its subject line matches, lowercased and stripped of punctuation. Nobody reads the body. That is the right trade on an incident morning and it is why triage keeps up.

It also means a ticket is only ever as precise as its subject lines. “Date is wrong” is the subject people reach for whatever went wrong with their date, so several faults with one symptom collapse into one thread. The decision records this consequence, but names it the other way round: different subjects splitting one bug. The reverse — one subject hiding several bugs — costs more, because it is invisible. The ticket looks handled.

The two

Each is filed as its own ticket, with the number of reports it covers:

  • The shared parser reads month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so dates come out flipped. This is the bug the desk already knows, and the queued fix is the right fix for it.
  • The booking-v3 form prints an MM/DD/YYYY hint. Its tenants are day-first. Their people type what the hint asks for, and the form then reads it the tenant’s way. Same symptom, different fault, and the parser fix does not touch it. Only reports through the booking form, and only from the three tenants on that form.

And one thing counted inside the first

A handful of reports describe a one-day move rather than a month and day swapping places: dates off by exactly one day, with daylight saving and time zones underneath. The desk had already spotted these and keeps their ids in desk-notes.json. They stay counted under the parser row and footnoted, as the desk counts them.

They are worth reading before the parser fix is called done. One describes an invite that arrived before its own booking was made. Another describes a single meeting held twice, because two cities saw two different days. Neither of those can be a month-first parse, and neither will be fixed by correcting one.

What intake cannot tell you

Intake guesses a cause from the reporter’s browser locale at the moment a report arrives. It never looks up which tenant sent it or which booking form they run, and those are what decide the cause. The table above counts how often that guess is wrong. It is wrong in both directions, so it cannot be corrected by leaning one way.

What to change

  1. Split #4471 into one ticket per fault and keep the counts separate. Closing the parser fix should not close the other two.
  2. Have intake record the tenant and the form, not the browser’s guess. The cause follows from those two facts and causeOf already computes it.
  3. Leave decision 0001 as it stands — it is accepted and it is right for triage. Add the step after it: when a ticket passes a few hundred reports, count it by cause before calling it known.

Lessons

  • A big ticket is mostly one bug reported many times. Sometimes it is several, and the subject line cannot tell you which.
  • Merging early is cheap. Never re-reading the merge is what costs.
